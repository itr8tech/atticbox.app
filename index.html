Moments

Mark the moments inside every tape.

Pause, type a name, press Return. Each moment gets its own date, place, people and notes. Marks under the slider show what’s coming up, and the list follows along as the tape plays.

  • Times read 1:23, just as the player shows them, and “Use playhead” sets a start or end to the frame on screen.
  • Float the video in Picture in Picture, make it big, and keep logging.
  • Poster frames, preview sheets and a color timeline for every recording.

Captions and search

Search for what was said.

AtticBox writes captions for your recordings with Apple’s speech recognition, right on your Mac, then lets you search every word. Click a line to jump straight to it.

  • One search box covers titles, dates, people, places, tags, moments and dialogue.
  • Import SRT or WebVTT captions you already have, and correct any line by hand.
  • Search keeps working with the media drive unplugged, and from Spotlight too.

Faces

Find every face, then decide who’s who.

Apple’s Vision framework finds faces in your photos and in frames sampled across every tape. AtticBox gathers the ones that look alike and asks: is this June? Untick the mistakes and confirm a page at a time.

  • Nobody is named until you say so, and Undo takes back any decision.
  • Jump from a portrait to the exact moment it appears in the tape.
  • Confirmed faces feed people search, moments, Events and exports.

People and families

Families, the way families actually work.

Build a family tree from the people in your pictures: parents, partners, children and siblings, birth and married names, adoptions and step-families. Every person gets a page with a timeline of the photos and videos they’re in, and how old they were in each.

  • Friends, godparents and neighbours get a place too.
  • Dates as they really are: 1952, about 1952, before 1990, 1950 or 1955.
  • Take your tree to MacFamilyTree, Reunion, Gramps, Ancestry and other genealogy apps.

Places

Put every memory on the map.

Pin the lake house, the church, the old school. Search by name or address, or move the crosshair to the spot yourself and say how sure you are, from “this exact house” to “somewhere in the county”.

  • Locations are written into the photos and videos themselves, where Apple Photos and other apps find them.
  • Built on OpenStreetMap, with an offline map you can include in your gallery.
  • Each place lists its photos, tapes, moments, Events, and who was born, married or died there.

Privacy

Your family stays in your house.

AtticBox has no account, no cloud and no analytics. Face detection and speech recognition run on your Mac with Apple’s own frameworks. Your photos, recordings and family tree never leave it.

Nothing uploaded

No frames, audio, faces or names are sent anywhere. Your originals stay where they are, on your own drives.

No AtticBox account

Nothing to sign up for or sign in to, and no server that has to stay running for your archive to keep working.

No tracking

No analytics, ads or telemetry. AtticBox doesn’t count what you do or report what’s in your archive.

The only online parts are optional: map tiles from OpenFreeMap and place search from Photon. They receive the part of the map you’re looking at and the place names you type, nothing else. You can turn both off in Settings and keep working with coordinates.

Questions

Do I need to digitize my tapes first?

Yes. AtticBox organizes the video files and scans you already have: the folder a digitizing service sends back, files from a library’s memory lab, or recordings from a USB video converter. It reads MP4, MOV, M4V, MKV, AVI and MTS/M2TS videos, and JPEG, HEIC, PNG, TIFF and WebP photos.

Is anything uploaded?

No. Faces and captions are worked out on your Mac. The only online parts are optional map tiles and place search, which receive the area of the map you’re looking at and what you type into place search. You can turn them off in Settings. The first time you make captions in a new language, macOS may download Apple’s speech files for it.

Will AtticBox change my original files?

Only if you ask it to. Titles, dates, people and places live in AtticBox’s own catalog until you choose Write to Files. Then you see every change first, and AtticBox keeps a verified backup of each original before it touches it.

What happens to my archive if I stop using AtticBox?

It keeps working. Everything AtticBox writes uses open formats: metadata inside the photos and videos, captions as SRT and WebVTT, your family tree as GEDCOM, places as GeoJSON and KML, and plain JSON files describing everything else. The exported gallery is a web page that opens from the folder in any browser.

Do my relatives need AtticBox to see it?

No. Export a family gallery to a drive or a shared folder, and anyone can open it in Safari, Chrome or Firefox on a Mac or PC, with no internet connection. They can play the tapes with captions, search what was said, and browse people, Events, timelines and places.

Is AtticBox a genealogy app?

It’s a companion to one. AtticBox records families, names, dates and places so it can connect people to their photos and videos, and it exports GEDCOM 7, GEDCOM 5.5.1 and GEDZIP for MacFamilyTree, Reunion, Family Historian, Gramps, Ancestry and others. It doesn’t do research sources, citations or record hints.

Which Macs does it run on?

Macs with Apple silicon running macOS 26 or later. Captions depend on the languages Apple’s speech recognition supports on your Mac.
